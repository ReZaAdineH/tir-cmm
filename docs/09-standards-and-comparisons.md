# Standards Alignment and Comparisons

TIR-CMM is not intended to replace established cybersecurity, incident-response, resilience or maturity frameworks. It measures a narrower operational question: whether the organisation can act on a detected attack in time, with authority, and prove that capability.

The canonical website provides alignment and comparison material for:

- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0;
- NIST SP 800-61r3;
- ISO/IEC 27035-1:2023;
- C2M2;
- CMMC;
- MITRE D3FEND;
- RE&CT;
- SOC-CMM;
- SIM3;
- CISA Zero Trust Maturity Model;
- DORA and NIS2;
- TID-CMM.

The site explicitly states where another framework is the better instrument. Crosswalks should therefore be treated as translation and evidence support, not claims of equivalence.

TIR-CMM also uses response-oriented ontologies such as RE&CT and MITRE D3FEND where useful for response-action mapping, while remaining vendor-neutral.

Version 1.0 corrected the crosswalk to avoid attributing a Recovery phase to ISO/IEC 27035-1:2023, a Governance domain to SOC-CMM, or the retired revision-2 lifecycle to NIST SP 800-61r3.

For regulatory environments such as DORA and NIS2, TIR-CMM can provide evidence about testing, reporting-clock readiness, authority, continuity and exercised response capability, but it is **not itself a compliance model**.
